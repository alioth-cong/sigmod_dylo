\stitle{Exp-2c: What $\varepsilon$ controls.}
A deferred source is refreshed when its own modularity term drifts relatively by
more than $\varepsilon$, or when it disappears. Writing
$q_A=\mathrm{in}_A/2m-(\Sigma_A/2m)^2$ and $q_A^{\text{ref}}$ for its value when
the summary was last written, the predicate is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eps}
\text{refresh } A \iff
\bigl|q_A-q_A^{\text{ref}}\bigr| \,/\, \bigl|q_A^{\text{ref}}\bigr| \;\ge\; \varepsilon ,
\end{equation}
evaluated once per batch per outstanding source at $O(1)$, since both terms are
maintained incrementally. We set $\varepsilon=10^{-2}$.

Table~\ref{tab:eps} discharges the forward reference of
Section~\ref{sec:algo:batch} empirically. The outstanding redundancy is small in
absolute terms at every tolerance---at most $5$ members on Epinions and $4$ on
DBLP over streams of $41$ and $105$ batches, against graphs of $7.6\times10^{4}$
and $3.2\times10^{5}$ vertices---so the ledger never accumulates more than
$10^{-4}$ of either graph. Within that envelope $\varepsilon$ trades executed
cost against freshness: tightening it from $5\times10^{-2}$ to $10^{-5}$ cuts the
mean outstanding redundancy by $51\times$ on Epinions ($2.56\to0.05$) and
$42\times$ on DBLP ($1.67\to0.04$), at the price of $13.8\%$ more executed
re-summarization on Epinions ($.1132\to.1288$). On DBLP the same tightening costs
only $0.4\%$ ($.1120\to.1124$), so the price is graph-dependent and small in
both cases. The operative range is narrow because the drift being compared is
itself small: over the surviving deferred sources the median of the left-hand
side of Eq.~\ref{eq:eps} is $5.8\times10^{-3}$ on Epinions and
$1.8\times10^{-3}$ on DBLP, with maxima of $1.98\times10^{-2}$ and
$4.66\times10^{-3}$; a tolerance of $5\times10^{-2}$ sits above the entire
observed distribution and never fires, which is what the drift-flush column
shows.

\emph{Most refreshes are not driven by $\varepsilon$ at all.} A source that loses
its last member is deleted rather than rewritten, and it is its parent's closure
that is charged; that traffic---$3{,}648$ and $4{,}870$ events---is independent of
the tolerance and dominates the $0$--$5$ drift-triggered refreshes by three
orders of magnitude. We therefore present $\varepsilon$ as a guardrail with a
measured bound rather than as a knob that buys throughput.

\emph{The metric matters.} The last row of Table~\ref{tab:eps} scores the same
runs by inferring cost from partitions rather than reading the schedule the
system emits. That scorer charges a source community as soon as its member set
changes, whether or not the refresh was executed, and therefore reports a
constant $\mathsf C(D)$ across the entire sweep---no trade-off, no effect of
$\varepsilon$. Any system whose contribution is a refresh \emph{schedule} rather
than a partition has to be measured this way, and we report both columns so the
difference is visible.

\emph{Scope.} The configuration evaluated everywhere else in this paper
materializes sources and destinations in the same batch, so no source is deferred
and $K_T\equiv0$ by construction; $\varepsilon$ is not exercised there.
Table~\ref{tab:eps} measures the deferring configuration. Numbers are
\DyLo{}-Leiden with singleton reclamation enabled, as in Table~\ref{tab:main};
the other two instances share the deferral path and were not separately swept.
