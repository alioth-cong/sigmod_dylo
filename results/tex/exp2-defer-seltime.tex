\stitle{Exp-2b: The single-anchor reduction, measured.}
Deferring removal-only sources leaves one anchor per action, and the exact
selector then collapses from a max-weight-closure solve to the tree program of
Theorem~\ref{thm:dp}. Table~\ref{tab:defer-seltime} measures that collapse
directly. With deferral enabled every round takes the DP path---$2{,}043$ of
$2{,}043$ across the four streams---and selection time falls by $77\%$ on DBLP,
$90\%$ on Epinions, $90\%$ on Flickr and $97.5\%$ on YouTube. Pooling all rounds,
a min-cut round costs $0.634$\,ms against the DP's $0.0086$\,ms, a $73.9\times$
gap that widens with graph size: on YouTube the two are $0.861$ and
$0.0128$\,ms.

\emph{This is invisible in the end-to-end numbers, and we report it separately
for that reason.} Selection accounts for $0.05\%$--$1.9\%$ of per-batch latency;
the remainder is graph maintenance, certificate upkeep and index emission. Total
per-batch latency is therefore unchanged to within seed noise
($4.28$ against $4.38$\,ms on Epinions, $26.4$ against $26.4$ on YouTube), which
is why Table~\ref{tab:main} does not separate the two configurations. Reporting
selection time alone in the main table would not be meaningful either: the
incremental baselines solve no cost-aware selection problem, so the phase has no
counterpart there.

\emph{Deferral does not raise cost.} Read from the refresh schedule the driver
emits, $\mathsf C(D)$ is bit-identical between the two configurations on $23$ of
$24$ (graph, instance) pairs, and differs by $+0.01\%$ on the remaining one
(Epinions/\Louvain{}); modularity agrees to five decimals throughout. We
highlight this because a scorer that infers cost from partitions alone reaches
the opposite conclusion---it charges a source community as soon as its member set
changes, whether or not that refresh was actually executed---and reports
increases of up to $44.8\%$ that never occur. The distinction matters for any
system whose contribution is a refresh \emph{schedule} rather than a partition.
