\stitle{Exp-8: Answer quality end to end.}
\DyLo{} regenerates $394$ of the $1{,}465$ communities it maintains and spends
$2.51$M input tokens on the ingested batch; the six incremental baselines
regenerate between $777$ and $985$ and a rebuild regenerates all of them. Judged
blind against all seven alternatives at once (Table~\ref{tab:arena-main}),
\DyLo{} leads the quality scale under both judges by a margin that clears every
other method's interval, and its win probability against each individual method
has a lower bound above $50\%$---from $64.9\%$ $[54.4,74.4]$ against a rebuild
to $74.7\%$ $[65.1,82.2]$ against $\Delta$-screening. The seven others span
$+0.060$ to $-0.408$ with intervals that overlap heavily, and we do not claim
they are separable from one another on this question set.

\emph{The lead is not an artifact of verbosity.} \DyLo{}'s answers are the
longest of the eight (median $2{,}538$ characters against $1{,}750$--$2{,}058$),
and the judges do reward length: fitting a conditional-logit Bradley--Terry with
a per-comparison length covariate,
$\Pr(i \succ j \mid q) = \sigma(s_i - s_j + \beta\,\Delta_{ijq})$ where
$\Delta_{ijq}$ is the standardized log-length difference, gives
$\beta = +4.76$ $[3.94,5.83]$ under GPT-5.6~Sol and $+5.84$ $[5.06,7.05]$ under
Opus~5---one standard deviation of log-length difference multiplies the odds of
winning by two orders of magnitude. The covariate is identified because the
length ordering is not fixed: \DyLo{} writes the longest answer on only $15$ of
$60$ questions and is shorter than each individual baseline on $25$--$35\%$ of
them. Holding length fixed halves \DyLo{}'s advantage but does not remove it:
its strength falls from $+0.804$ to $+0.405$ $[+0.05,+0.72]$ under GPT-5.6~Sol
and from $+0.846$ to $+0.501$ $[+0.13,+0.87]$ under Opus~5, remaining first and
significantly positive under both.

We report both numbers rather than only the adjusted one, because length here is
a \emph{mediator} rather than a confound: \DyLo{}'s answers are longer precisely
because its index exposes $558$ reports at $\mathrm{level}\le 1$ against
$453$--$486$ for every other arm, so global search has more evidence to
synthesize. Conditioning on a post-treatment variable understates the total
effect. The unadjusted strength answers ``is it worth deploying \DyLo{}'', the
adjusted one answers ``is the advantage only verbosity''; both are positive.

\emph{Question style changes the middle of the ranking, not the top.}
We repeated the whole protocol on a second, independently generated set of $60$
questions (Appendix~\ref{app:questions}). Within either question set the judges
agree closely---Kendall's $\tau$ is $+0.929$ between the two judges on the first
set and $+1.000$, $+0.929$, $+0.929$ among three judges on the second---but the
same judge reorders the middle when the question style changes
($\tau = +0.357$ for GPT-5.6~Sol and $+0.286$ for Opus~5 across the two sets).
The moves are systematic rather than random and both judges make the same ones:
\QCA{} rises from seventh to third, DF-\Louvain{} from fifth to second, while a
rebuild falls from second to fifth and HIT-Leiden from sixth to last. The first
set asks for cross-community synthesis (``how has X changed'', ``what patterns''),
the second for location and enumeration (``identify the top five'', ``determine
which category''), and an index tuned for one is not automatically better for
the other. \DyLo{} is first on every judge and both question sets---the only
invariant across all five evaluations---so we report the synthesis set in
Table~\ref{tab:arena-main} and the enumeration set in
Table~\ref{tab:arena-appendix}, rather than averaging two genuinely different
results into one.
