\section{Graph-benchmark protocols and diagnostics}\label{app:benchmarks}

These experiments isolate structural refresh work and CPU graph maintenance.
Section~\ref{app:exp5-detail} separately measures actual LLM time and tokens.

\subsection{Benchmark domains}\label{app:dataset-details}

The ten SNAP/Network Data Repository graphs~\cite{snapnets,nr} comprise
social networks (Facebook, GPlus, Twitter, Flickr, YouTube, LiveJournal),
a trust network (Epinions), citation networks (Cit-HepPh, Cit-HepTh), and
a co-authorship network (DBLP). We symmetrize directed relations, remove
self-loops, merge duplicate undirected edges, and count incident vertices.
The resulting LiveJournal graph has $4{,}846{,}609$ vertices and
$42{,}851{,}237$ edges.

The initial graph contains $90\%$ of edges; the remaining $10\%$ arrive
in batches of up to $1{,}000$ edges. Full streams contain $9$, $36$, $43$,
$41$, $135$, $105$, $1{,}224$, $320$, and $299$ batches for Facebook,
Cit-HepTh, Cit-HepPh, Epinions, Twitter, DBLP, GPlus, Flickr, and YouTube,
respectively. LiveJournal uses the first $200$ of $4{,}286$ batches.
Incremental methods retain the initialized partition; from-scratch (FS)
references rerun the detector after every batch. DyLo wraps Louvain, SLM,
or Leiden. Competitors are DynaMo, Batch, GreMod, QCA, $\Delta$-screening,
DF-Louvain, DF-Leiden, HIT-Leiden, and reuse-aware rebuild. The last uses
the FS Leiden partition while retaining unchanged reports.

\subsection{Execution and accounting defaults}\label{app:exp-defaults}

Graph benchmarks run memory-resident and single-threaded on Ubuntu~22.04,
an AMD EPYC~9654, and $1.1$\,TiB RAM. DyLo and the four localized baselines
use C++17 (GCC~11.4, \texttt{-O3 -march=native}); DynaMo, Batch, GreMod,
and QCA use OpenJDK~21. Python~3.10 accounting runs offline.
For $T$ updates, stream-mean modularity is
$\bar Q=T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T}Q_t$, excluding the initial graph.
Latency and structural cost are also averaged over batches, then equally
over repeated runs. Main comparisons and component ablations use five-run
means where applicable; LiveJournal uses one run and selector timings use
three-seed medians.

Structural weights count entities at leaves and children internally
($r_{\mathrm{int}}=1$), normalized so a complete reset costs $1$.
They cover community-structure changes; evidence-only refreshes and LLM
execution are outside this metric. Schedule-based ablations charge executed
work plus closing minus opening pending liability
(Eq.~\ref{eq:supp-ledger}). Graph-maintenance latency excludes parsing,
partition output, and LLM generation; selector driver timings include output.
Defaults are $\lambda=0.1$ and source deferral with $\varepsilon=0.05$;
the 32B case study retains its archived eager-source policy.

All DyLo runs prune using current unpaid marginal weights $\bar w_t$.
Across three detector instances on Facebook, Cit-HepTh, Cit-HepPh,
Epinions, Twitter, and DBLP, the maximum seed range is
$3.3\times10^{-6}$ in $\bar Q$ and $1.0\%$ of mean structural cost.
Latency ranges reach $48\%$ on Facebook and remain below $5\%$ on DBLP.
GPlus, Flickr, YouTube, and LiveJournal combine separate latency and
structural-cost series.

\stitle{LiveJournal scope.} FS Leiden takes $74$ hours for the measured
$200$ batches; FS Louvain and SLM were not run. Baseline costs are recomputed
from saved partitions restricted to currently incident vertices.
DynaMo's normalized cost is $0.990302$. Peak memory is $9.5$\,GB for each
DyLo instance and $4.2$--$54.5$\,GB for the baselines.

\stitle{Comparable cost units.} Partition-only baselines charge changed
leaf member sets and normalize by current leaf weight. DyLo charges its
hierarchy refreshes and pending balance, normalized by full hierarchy weight.
The represented depths differ, so these normalized fractions are not directly
ordered by their scope. A common leaf-only LiveJournal check gives
$0.009723$, $0.009705$, and $0.009734$ for DyLo-Louvain, SLM, and Leiden,
versus $0.628814$ for GreMod, the least expensive baseline in that scope.
These seed-$42$ cold-start traces average updates $1$--$200$, normalize by
current active vertices, exclude evidence-only refreshes, and have zero
pending DyLo liability at every batch boundary.

\subsection{Recurring and shared refresh measurements}\label{app:motivation}

We replay HIT-Leiden partitions on the first $40$ batches of nine streams
excluding LiveJournal (all $9$ for Facebook and $36$ for Cit-HepTh).
Repeatedly contracting the flat partition supplies a hierarchy, with
entity counts at leaves and child counts internally.
Under simulated eager refresh, a source losing a member refreshes its
root path. Refreshing a node already refreshed for this reason in an earlier
batch is recurring work; it accounts for $44$--$96\%$ of source-side cost.
For accepted moves $a$, shared-path multiplicity is
\[
\frac{\sum_a w(\mathrm{Anc}(\mathrm{src}(a))\cup
                         \mathrm{Anc}(\mathrm{dst}(a)))}
 {w(\bigcup_a[\mathrm{Anc}(\mathrm{src}(a))\cup
                         \mathrm{Anc}(\mathrm{dst}(a))])},
\]
where $w(S)=\sum_{c\in S}w(c)$. Window means range from $5.9$ to $1{,}062$,
quantifying duplicate charges when paths are priced separately.

\stitle{Identical-input solver check.} Synthetic forests maximize
$\sum_{c\in D}(g(c)-\lambda\bar w_t(c))$ over ancestor-closed $D$, with
fixed marginal weights, gains from $U(-10,10)$, and
$\lambda\in\{0,0.1,0.5,1,2\}$. Enumeration, min-cut closure, and DP
agree on all $5{,}000$ tests with at most $22$ nodes; DP and closure agree
through $20{,}000$ nodes. At the latter size, mean solve times are
$1.2407$\,ms for DP and $5.6968$\,ms for closure. This check holds the
optimization instance fixed, whereas toggling deferral changes it.

\stitle{Timing and ledger boundaries.} Selection counters accumulate over
the full stream; driver times are per batch and include index emission.
A time share requires a common denominator. The deferral cost comparison
uses emitted refresh plans and Eq.~\ref{eq:supp-ledger}, with $P_0=0$,
across eight graphs and three detector instances. Costs agree on $23/24$
pairs; Epinions/Louvain differs by $+0.01\%$. Every pair agrees in
$\bar Q$ to five decimal places, with unsettled work still charged.

The component ablation uses its own internal control and the same ledger,
independently removing certificates, incremental cost-tree maintenance,
or pruning on Epinions, Flickr, and YouTube. Removing either of the first
two preserves partitions and structural scores. Removing pruning can
change committed sets and later trajectories while preserving the optimum
value for a fixed candidate set.

\subsection{Batch size and ingestion delay}\label{app:exp3-detail}

Batch sizes $E\in\{1,10,100,1000\}$ are evaluated on a graph extracted
from MultiHop-RAG news ($24.2$K edges; stream/base ratio $.91$) and
Cit-HepPh ($420.9$K edges; ratio $.11$). The measured ingestion rate used
for the delay axis is $r_{\mathrm{in}}=6.04$ edges/s. The plotted
continuous-arrival approximation is $E/(2r_{\mathrm{in}})$;
equally spaced arrivals have exact mean wait $(E-1)/(2r_{\mathrm{in}})$,
including zero for $E=1$. This wait ends at batch closure, before graph
maintenance and report generation.

The rate-$0$ news curve shows batching amortization; at the default
$\lambda=0.1$, singleton processing already costs $0.611$ structural units
per edge, versus $113.5$ at rate $0$. Cost-aware selection therefore
reduces the work left for batching to amortize. Lazy evaluation lowers
singleton cost by $43\%$ on Cit-HepPh and has negligible effect on the
news graph, where the large increment leaves less state reusable.

\subsection{Exchange-rate sweep}\label{app:exp4-detail}

We sweep $\lambda\in\{0,10^{-3},10^{-2},10^{-1},0.3,1,3,10,\infty\}$
over the full Cit-HepPh, Cit-HepTh, DBLP, Epinions, Facebook, and Flickr
streams. Cost ratios and modularity losses compare each run with the same
detector's rate-$0$ run: $\mathrm{Cost}(\lambda)/\mathrm{Cost}(0)$ and
$\bar Q(0)-\bar Q(\lambda)$. Each rate generates its own trajectory,
whereas Proposition~\ref{prop:mono} holds candidates fixed.

All six curves flatten from $10^{-2}$ onward. Increasing the rate from
$10^{-2}$ to $10$ changes normalized structural cost by at most $0.0045$
and modularity by less than $10^{-4}$, supporting the shared default $0.1$.
Facebook retains a cost ratio of $0.402$ at the knee; Epinions has the
largest modularity loss, $0.67\times10^{-3}$. The Epinions modularity
values $0.441$, $0.445$, and $0.458$ refer respectively to FS Louvain,
FS/reuse-aware Leiden, and this cost-oblivious sweep configuration.

\section{32B GraphRAG maintenance and sensemaking}\label{app:exp5-detail}

This case study compares DyLo, Batch, Rebuild, HIT-Leiden, DynaMo, QCA,
DF-Louvain, and $\Delta$-screen on one frozen graph update. It measures
re-summarization and publication time, successful update-summary tokens,
and synthesis quality on the resulting queryable indices.

\subsection{Frozen workload and report reuse}\label{app:case-workload}

Documents come from MultiHop-RAG's English news corpus~\cite{tang2024multihoprag}.
Every method starts from the same archived $304$-document index and receives
the same $23$-document update. The final extracted graph contains $327$
documents, $13{,}264$ entities, $12{,}925$ relationships, and $833$ text units.
Its semantic SHA-256 is
\texttt{cee9752bf403ba839}\allowbreak\texttt{0692cb8928ad5ce0}\allowbreak
\texttt{d3037983b3778a86}\allowbreak\texttt{fbf2caca8c196f9}.

DyLo uses the archived \texttt{std4} binary and partition, with
$\lambda=0.1$, $\sigma=0.2$, hierarchy parameter $\gamma=5$, and eager
source refresh. Baselines use their native adapters, including native
partition initialization where required. All $1{,}554$ initial reports,
including $134$ previously missing reports, are generated with
Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct~\cite{yang2024qwen25} before maintenance.
For each updated community, incremental methods reuse the first available
old report with the same entity-membership set. This rule can retain
earlier evidence when content changes within unchanged membership.
Only communities with internal edges are materialized; every materialized
community has a report. Rebuild regenerates all reports.

\subsection{Generation, timing, and token accounting}\label{app:case-accounting}

Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct runs in BF16 with an English structured prompt requiring
five findings, a $2{,}048$-token output cap, seed $17$, and four concurrent
requests. All eight arms run separately on the same H100 with identical
settings and pass resource-isolation checks. Response and prefix caching
are disabled. Source contexts have a $4{,}000$-token \texttt{cl100k\_base}
budget; usage is measured with Qwen's native tokenizer. The model context
limit is $16{,}384$ tokens and the model revision is
\texttt{5ede1c97bbab6ce5}\allowbreak\texttt{cda5812749b4c0bdf79b18dd}.

\subsection{Questions and sensemaking generation}\label{app:questions}

Following GraphRAG's user--task question-generation approach~\cite{edge2024graphrag},
the archived question bank was generated from a prose description of the
news corpus. We use its first $60$ questions, $q_0$--$q_{59}$, frozen before
generation on the new indices. They cover five user roles and $15$ tasks,
including temporal trends, journalistic practices, and business analysis.
For example, $q_0$ asks which entities are mentioned most frequently across
categories from September to December 2023; $q_{14}$ asks about viewpoint
diversity; $q_{20}$ asks which themes recur across categories.
The companion \texttt{artifacts/sensemaking-questions.csv} contains the exact
question text, IDs, roles, and tasks. Questions requesting unavailable
evidence are retained, including any resulting insufficient-evidence answers.

Each maintained index supplies its complete level-$0$ root-report cut to
GraphRAG global-search map--reduce. All methods use the same 32B English
answer generator, $8{,}000$-Qwen-token budgets per map chunk and for reduce
evidence, and output caps of $2{,}048$ map tokens and $3{,}072$ reduce tokens.
Map points carry usefulness scores; the reducer synthesizes useful points
into paragraphs. Native root cuts expose $151$ reports in eight chunks for
DyLo and $67$--$78$ reports in four chunks for each baseline
(Table~\ref{tab:sensemaking-costs}). This comparison uses equal per-call
budgets; total query work varies with the maintained index.

Structured outputs require a normal stop, complete valid JSON, and allowed
report identifiers. Tool markers and truncation trigger technical retries
with unchanged evidence/output budgets, a format-only instruction, and
compact JSON grammar. Valid checkpoints are retained; citations are rendered
mechanically. All $480$ final answers pass validation. Judge scores never
trigger answer regeneration.

\subsection{Independent judging and uncertainty}\label{app:case-judge}

seed $17$, low reasoning effort, and a $2{,}048$-token output cap.
Blind pairwise prompts allow ties and apply four GraphRAG criteria:
comprehensiveness (coverage), diversity (perspectives), empowerment (support
for informed judgment), and directness (focus on the question).
Each DyLo--baseline pair is assessed in both orders for every question and
criterion, giving $7\times60\times4\times2=3{,}360$ oriented cases.
Validated verdicts are reused for identical prompts.
