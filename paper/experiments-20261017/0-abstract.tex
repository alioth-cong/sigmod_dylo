\begin{abstract}

GraphRAG answers multi-hop and corpus-level questions from LLM-generated summaries of a hierarchical knowledge-graph community index. As the corpus evolves, each change in community membership invalidates the summaries along the affected hierarchy paths, and regenerating them---not graph computation---dominates maintenance cost: graph-side maintenance takes milliseconds per batch, whereas re-summarization takes minutes. Existing dynamic community-detection methods, however, accept any modularity-improving change regardless of the summaries it invalidates. We present DyLo, a framework that maintains the GraphRAG community index by pricing every structural change by the re-summarization work it induces. DyLo (i) refreshes summaries that gain members immediately, while deferring the consolidation of summaries that only lose members until their drift exceeds a bound; (ii) accounts for shared and deferred refresh work in a ledger that never charges the same work twice; and (iii) selects changes jointly with an exact linear-time tree DP. One shared maintenance layer supports Louvain, SLM, and Leiden. On ten real-world graphs, DyLo incurs the lowest re-summarization cost among incremental methods while staying within $0.01$ of from-scratch modularity. On a matched MultiHop-RAG graph update, it reduces re-summarization time by $44.0\%$ relative to Batch and $75.3\%$ relative to a full rebuild, and successful summary tokens by $27.2\%$ and $61.0\%$, respectively. On $60$ synthesis questions, an independent local judge favors its diversity over both comparators; other quality criteria show mixed preferences.

\end{abstract}
